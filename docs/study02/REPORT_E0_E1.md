# Study 02, Experiments 0 and 1: report

*2026-11-10. Companion to the [proposal](PROPOSAL.md) and the pre-registered plans for
[Experiment 0](../../experiments/study02_e0_bridge/PLAN.md) and
[Experiment 1](../../experiments/study02_e1_diversity/PLAN.md). Tables are generated by
`scripts/s2_analyze.py` into [`results/study02`](../../results/study02); nothing in them is edited by hand.*

## 1. Summary

**Recommendation: the evidence justifies Experiment 2, on the 1.5B student, after two pieces of preparation
(§8).** It does not justify running it on the 0.5B student as originally preferred.

| question | answer | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Does the PyTorch stack reproduce Study 01? | Yes | B and C within 0.8-2.3 points of Study 01 on `eval_id` and `eval_ood`; token counts identical on all 250 steps; 0 of 720 episodes change between two scoring jobs |
| Was the Study 01 gap an artefact of its training settings? | No | best `eval_ood` under conventional settings is 42.5%, against a 60% threshold |
| **H1 (0.5B):** does held-out success rise with the number of distinct training families at fixed episodes and steps? | **Supported** | `test_comp` 18.9% at 25 families, 37.7% at 1,600: **+18.9 points [15.7, 22.2]**; +3.1 points per doubling |
| H1 for the 1.5B student (secondary) | Supported | 50.0% to 76.9%: +26.9 points [22.2, 31.4] |
| How high can the 0.5B student get on this task space with oracle labels? | 37.7% [33.7, 41.5] on `test_comp` | below the 40% that the plan required for running Experiment 2 on it |
| And the 1.5B student? | 76.9% [73.8, 79.8] | |

What the results say beyond the pre-registered test:

- **Diversity closes the composition gap; it does not raise the depth limit.** With 25 training families the
  0.5B student solves 57% of new three-hop instances of its own families and 19% of three-hop held-out
  compositions. With 1,600 families the two numbers are 42% and 39%. At matched depth, a composition the model
  has never practised is then no harder than a family it has practised. What remains is depth: the 0.5B student
  solves 17-29% of four-hop tasks, in or out of distribution, and 7-13% of five- and six-hop tasks.
- **The effect is not only tool coverage.** On the test families whose tools and practised edges a 25-family
  training set already contains, success still rises from 34% to 46% (0.5B) and from 62% to 82% (1.5B) when the
  training set grows to 1,600 families (post-hoc).
- **Diversity costs the small model in-distribution accuracy.** Own-family success falls from 62% to 51% for the
  0.5B student as 3,200 episodes are spread over more families. The 1.5B student holds at 87-89%.
- **Transfer to Study 01's hand-written tasks is limited.** Trained only on generated tasks, the 0.5B student
  reaches 20% on the v1 anchor and the 1.5B student 52%, against 59% for Study 01's best arm, which was trained
  on v1.

Cost: **50.37 USD** of the 60 USD cap, as billed by the provider (§6). Two things did not go to plan and are
reported in full: the reference-model quality gate could vouch only for shallow test families (§4.3), and
scoring cost three times the estimate, so the baselines and the 1.5B student were scored on fewer splits than
planned (§5.5).

### 2.1 Three details of the port that matter

1. **MLX's Adam applies no bias correction by default.** Study 01's `lr 1e-5` therefore took steps two to six
   times larger than PyTorch's AdamW would at the same setting. The reproduction uses the uncorrected update;
   the conventional settings use the standard one.
2. **The checkpoint ships with sampling switched on** (`do_sample`, temperature 0.7, repetition penalty 1.1).
   MLX ignored those defaults; `transformers` applies them unless told otherwise. The port switches them off.
3. **MLX-LM's `scale` is not PEFT's `alpha`.** Scale 20 at rank 16 is `lora_alpha = 320`.

Checks made on CPU before any GPU was used, all part of the test suite:

- With the real tokenizer and weights, the port sees the same number of tokens and supervised tokens as the
  Study 01 log on **all 250 steps** of variants B and C, and computes the same loss before any update
  (0.5565 against 0.5562 for C, 0.4104 against 0.4122 for B).
- 5,865,472 trainable parameters for the Study 01 layout, as reported.
- Loss covers assistant tokens only; a packed episode equals its per-turn examples; batched greedy decoding
  equals unbatched; an interrupted run resumes to the same adapter; the optimizer matches a reference
  implementation in both modes and equals `torch.optim.AdamW` in the corrected mode.

### 3.1 Reproduction (step B): passed

Three seeds each of B (teacher demonstrations) and C (oracle trajectories), with Study 01's settings in PEFT
terms, scored on the Study 01 evaluation files.

| | `eval_id` | `eval_ood` | `eval_unseen` | `eval_renamed` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B, this port (mean of 3) | 85.2 | 31.4 | 47.8 | 71.2 |
| B, Study 01 (MLX) | 86.0 | 30.3 | 33.3 | 73.3 |
| C, this port (mean of 3) | 92.0 | 29.4 | 39.4 | 74.8 |

The criterion was the two left-hand columns within 5 points and every run stable; the largest difference there
is 2.3 points and all six runs pass the stability rule. `eval_unseen` is 14 points higher for B than in
Study 01. That split has four templates and its per-seed values range from 31% to 56% here, as the
[audit](STUDY01_AUDIT.md) said they would; it was not part of the criterion and I do not read it as a
difference between the stacks.

On identical batches the loss follows the Study 01 log step for step, while the gradient norm before clipping
does not:

| step | loss, this port | loss, Study 01 | gradient norm, this port | gradient norm, Study 01 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5542 | 0.5562 | 62.4 | 854.6 |
| 5 | 0.2818 | 0.2827 | 27.1 | 213.5 |
| 10 | 0.2372 | 0.2481 | 9.8 | 72.7 |
| 20 | 0.0626 | 0.0764 | 8.5 | 10.0 |
| 30 | 0.0138 | 0.0122 | 2.9 | 2.9 |
| 50 | 0.0062 | 0.0089 | 1.2 | 3.8 |

Both runs clip to norm 1 and Adam rescales, so the trajectories agree, but at initialisation the MLX gradient
was 14 times larger for the same loss on the same batch. A plausible cause is precision: this port keeps the
weights in float32 and computes in bfloat16 under autocast, which performs the attention softmax in float32,
while the MLX run computed everything in bfloat16. I have not verified that. It matters for how Study 01's
stability problems are read (next section).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5e-05 | 250 | 88.0 | 87.5 | 17.1 | yes |

- **All six runs are stable**, including all blocks with `alpha = 2 x rank` at lr 1e-4, the configuration that

### 3.3 Repeatability (step D) and throughput (step A)

- Scoring the same adapter in two separate jobs changed the outcome of **0 of 720 episodes**.
- Training runs at about 10,000 tokens per second on both GPU types with the Study 01 layout, and 7,500 with
  all blocks. The L4 is cheaper per training token (0.022 against 0.027 USD per million). The A10G scores 1.7
  times faster, and scoring dominates the cost of a run, so Experiment 1 used the A10G. This departs from the
  letter of the step A rule and is logged as a deviation.

### 3.4 Decision

Every stop/go condition for proceeding is met: reproduction within 5 points, best conventional `eval_ood`
below 60%, flip rate below 2%, cost below the estimate (1.9 GPU-hours and about 2.1 USD against 5 GPU-hours
and 4 USD). Experiment 1 was run with rank 16, alpha 32, all blocks, bias-corrected AdamW, lr 1e-4, two
passes over the data.


## 4. The v2 environment

### 4.1 Schema, tools and families

One schema lists 12 domains, 37 entity types, their attributes and 60 references between them. From it
`ccl/world2.py` derives **122 tools** (37 look-ups by ID, 18 searches by name, 39 list tools, 20
state-changing actions, 8 back-up look-ups), each simulated by one of five generic functions. No domain, tool
or identifier prefix is shared with Study 01. The proposal expected about 80 tools; the schema needed more
references than first drafted to give a practice space of 5,000 families, and each listable reference adds a
tool.

A **family** is one gold dependency graph: the role, the expected status, the calls with their
dependencies, and the faults. It is Study 01's graph signature with one refinement: a tool that fails once
(retry) and a tool that is down (fallback) are different families. Study 01's signature merges them, which is
why its 24 templates had 23 signatures.

What does and does not make a new family is fixed in `ccl/compose.py` and enforced by tests:

- Two families always differ in which tools are called, in how the calls depend on each other, or in the
  behaviour required (retry, fallback, stop on not-found, stop on a denied approval, refuse for lack of role).
- Wording, concrete values, the attribute asked for, the rule that picks a record from a list (lowest, highest,
  matching), distractor tools and the order of independent calls never make a new family.
- A base graph has at most one retry variant and one fallback variant; the failing call is fixed by a hash, so
  the position of a fault does not multiply families.
- "The same question about two records at once" is a family only for paths of at most two calls.
- Requests about a record that does not exist share one family per first call, whatever would have followed.

### 4.2 Splits

Hold-outs are assigned by hash, not by hand. Two domains (insurance, property) are reserved for
`test_domain` and one (hotel) for `dev`; 8 tools for `test_tool` and 4 for `dev`; 63 of the 317 edges between
practice tools for `test_comp` and 25 for `dev`. An edge is held out only if both of its tools stay practised
through other edges. A tool belongs to every domain it touches, so a practice task cannot even list records by
the ID of a held-out entity.

| split | definition | families (base graphs) | tasks | hops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| practice space | 1-4 hops over practised tools and edges | 5,364 (2,103) | generated on demand | 408 / 758 / 1,320 / 2,878 |
| `test_comp` (**primary**) | at least one edge never practised, practised tools only, 2-4 hops | 600 (453) | 1,200 | 182 / 206 / 212 |
| `test_depth` | 5-6 hops over practised tools and edges | 200 | 400 | 100 / 100 |
| `test_tool` | needs a tool absent from every practice catalogue | 200 | 400 | 29 / 55 / 58 / 58 |
| `test_domain` | two domains never practised | 200 | 400 | 50 / 54 / 51 / 45 |
| `test_id` | new instances of practice families | 200 | 400 | 49 / 51 / 50 / 50 |
| `dev` | its own held-out edges, tools and domain | 300 | 600 | 30 / 91 / 95 / 84 |
| v1 anchor | Study 01's four evaluation files, frozen | 44 templates | 720 | |

Of the practice families, 2,365 have no modifier, 2,089 are retry variants, 586 fallback, 139 denied approval,
180 not-found, 4 role refusals and 1 needs no tool. Evaluation families are drawn with the same fixed modifier
mix as the training subsets (about 60% none, 18% retry, 11% fallback, 8% denial). About 46% of evaluation tasks
use at least one phrase variant that training never shows.

### 4.3 Controls on validity

| risk | control | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| distinct families that are really the same structure | family identity is the gold graph; one fault variant per graph; twins only for short paths | `tests/test_v2.py` |
| difficulty drifting with the number of families | every nested subset has the same mix of hops and modifiers | 2.8-2.9 gold calls per task at every level; table in §5 |
| D confounded with tool and edge coverage | not removable by design; both recorded per run, and success reported for families the run's training set covers | §5.3 |
| test structure leaking into training | one split per family; training reads `data/v2/practice` only; scoring is a separate script | `test_no_held_out_structure_reaches_training_inputs` |
| test instances leaking into training | disjoint ID ranges and name lists for training and evaluation instances | 0 shared record IDs and 0 shared requests in the build's audit |
| tuning on test data | settings fixed in Experiment 0 on Study 01's validation tasks; nothing tuned in Experiment 1; every access to a sealed split logged | `results/study02/test_access.jsonl` |
| tasks that penalise defensible behaviour | oracle and degenerate-policy gate; manual read of about 100 requests; reference-model gate | `data/v2/gate/README.md` |
| the generator's style being what is learned | v1 anchor (hand-written tasks and tools, never trained on); phrase variants reserved for evaluation | §5.2 |

Two defects were found and fixed by reading generated tasks before the data were frozen: a fan-out could
overwrite the reference that a list had just used (an inconsistent world, caught because the oracle then used
an ID it had not observed), and nested requests were ambiguous about which noun a selection rule applied to
(every phrase now ends in the noun phrase it builds on, so requests are right-branching). A later read found
that a question could ask for the attribute its own rule had stated, and that a list tool keyed by a held-out
entity was still a practice tool; both were fixed before freezing.

The reference-model step of the quality gate turned out weaker than planned and is described honestly in
[`data/v2/gate/README.md`](../../data/v2/gate/README.md): `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` solved 3% of four-hop tasks
through its own errors, so it could vouch only for shallow families. No family was removed.

## 5. Experiment 1: diversity dose-response

Plan, clarifications, frozen inputs and deviations:
[`experiments/study02_e1_diversity/PLAN.md`](../../experiments/study02_e1_diversity/PLAN.md). Full tables:
[`results/study02/e1/tables.md`](../../results/study02/e1/tables.md); post-hoc analyses:
[`posthoc.md`](../../results/study02/e1/posthoc.md).

### 5.1 Design as run

Twenty-six adapters were trained from the base models on oracle trajectories: 3,200 episodes and 800 steps of
8 episodes each at every level, differing only in how many distinct families the episodes come from (25, 100,
400 or 1,600, with 128, 32, 8 or 2 instances per family). Five replicates per level for the 0.5B student; three
replicates at 25 and 1,600 for the 1.5B student. Replicate *r* draws its own nested family subsets and uses
seed *r*. All 26 runs pass the stability rule. Each training set was rebuilt inside its GPU job and matched the
SHA-256 recorded in its config.

What a level changes besides D is measured, not assumed away:

| D | base graphs | gold tools (of 79) | practised edges (of 229) | gold calls per task | hops 1/2/3/4 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 34 | 43 | 39 | 2.95 | 16 / 28 / 28 / 28 |
| 100 | 123 | 69 | 110 | 2.94 | 18 / 25 / 28 / 29 |
| 400 | 465 | 77 | 198 | 2.92 | 18 / 25 / 28 / 29 |
| 1,600 | 1,383 | 79 | 227 | 2.93 | 18 / 25 / 28 / 29 |

Depth and compositional complexity are held constant across levels. Tool and edge coverage are not: a set of
25 families cannot show 79 tools. §5.3 deals with that.

### 5.2 Confirmatory result: H1 is supported

Success on `test_comp`, 600 held-out families with two tasks each, every family containing at least one edge
that no practice family contains. Intervals are 95%, from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of replicates, families
and tasks.

| distinct training families | 0.5B student | replicate means | 1.5B student | replicate means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 18.9 [15.8, 21.9] | 21.4, 21.2, 14.8, 19.2, 17.6 | 50.0 [46.0, 53.8] | 47.8, 52.2, 50.1 |
| 100 | 28.1 [25.2, 31.0] | 28.8, 26.8, 27.6, 29.8, 27.8 | | |
| 400 | 33.8 [28.7, 39.3] | 42.2, 28.7, 30.8, 36.8, 30.3 | | |
| 1,600 | 37.7 [33.7, 41.5] | 39.8, 37.5, 32.9, 37.8, 40.5 | 76.9 [73.8, 79.8] | 77.8, 75.2, 77.7 |
| **1,600 minus 25** | **+18.9 [+15.7, +22.2]** | | **+26.9 [+22.2, +31.4]** | |

- **H1 (0.5B), the single confirmatory test: supported.** The difference is at least 10 points and its interval
  excludes zero. Every one of the five replicates shows it (smallest difference +16.3).
- The trend is +3.1 points per doubling of D [2.6, 3.7], with diminishing returns: +9.2, +5.7 and +3.9 points
  for each fourfold increase.
- The same contrast for the 1.5B student (secondary): supported, and larger.
- Sensitivity check, mixed-effects logistic regression with family and replicate as random effects
  ([`glmm.json`](../../results/study02/e1/glmm.json)): odds ratio for 1,600 against 25 families 4.3 [3.9, 4.7]
  for the 0.5B student and 6.1 [5.4, 6.9] for the 1.5B student.
- Untrained baselines score 0.0-0.2% on `test_comp` for both sizes, with or without the few-shot prompt.

Other splits, 0.5B student (success %):

| split | D = 25 | D = 100 | D = 400 | D = 1,600 | 1,600 minus 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `test_tool` (a tool never shown) | 26.6 | 36.5 | 40.8 | 43.0 | +16.4 [+11.1, +21.8] |
| `test_domain` (two domains never shown) | 36.0 | 47.1 | 51.7 | 55.8 | +19.8 [+14.3, +25.6] |
| `test_depth` (5-6 hops) | 1.1 | 3.5 | 6.3 | 10.0 | +8.9 [+5.6, +12.3] |
| `test_id` (practice families, new instances) | 30.9 | 45.8 | 48.2 | 50.9 | +20.0 [+14.8, +25.2] |
| `dev` | 26.3 | 38.9 | 42.6 | 46.5 | +20.2 [+14.2, +26.5] |
| own training families, new instances | 62.5 | 52.5 | 50.0 | 51.4 | -11.1 |
| v1 anchor (720 hand-written tasks) | 12.7 | 16.1 | 21.6 | 19.7 | +7.0 |

`test_domain` and `test_tool` look easier than `test_comp` only because they include one-hop families; at
matched depth they are within a few points of each other (§5.4). The 1.5B student reaches 81.4% on `test_id`,
86.9% on its own families and 52.3% on the v1 anchor at D = 1,600.

### 5.3 Is it diversity, or just coverage of tools and edges?

The pre-registered secondary endpoint restricts `test_comp` to families whose tools all occur in the run's own
training set. At 25 families that is 30% of the test families, and the 0.5B student scores 23.6% on them
against 37.7% at 1,600: **+14.1 [+9.6, +18.0]**. Requiring the practised edges to be covered as well leaves 17%
of the families and a difference of +4.1 [-2.4, +9.9], but that compares different families at the two levels,
and the covered ones are the easy ones.

The like-for-like comparison (post-hoc) scores every level on the same two groups of families, defined by what
each replicate's 25-family set covers:

| test families | per replicate | 0.5B: D = 25 | D = 100 | D = 400 | D = 1,600 | 1.5B: D = 25 | D = 1,600 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tools and practised edges already covered at D = 25 | about 105 | 34.4 | 37.4 | 43.5 | 45.5 | 62.4 | 81.6 |
| not covered at D = 25 | about 495 | 15.8 | 26.3 | 31.8 | 36.1 | 47.4 | 75.8 |

On families whose ingredients the small training set already contains, more families still help: +11 points
for the 0.5B student (every replicate positive, +7.5 to +17.7) and +19 for the 1.5B student. On the rest the
gain is +20 and +28. A substantial part of the effect therefore survives when coverage is held fixed, and the
rest comes with coverage. Both are things a more diverse practice space provides, but only the first is
"learning to compose". The two groups differ in difficulty, so the sizes of the two gains should not be read as
an exact split.

### 5.4 Where the remaining failures are: depth, not novelty

Success by number of hops, 0.5B student, replicates pooled (post-hoc):

| | hops | D = 25 | D = 1,600 |
|---|---|---|---|
| own training families | 2 / 3 / 4 | 73 / 57 / 42 | 66 / 42 / 29 |
| `test_id` (practice families) | 2 / 3 / 4 | 38 / 18 / 6 | 62 / 41 / 17 |
| `test_comp` (unpractised edge) | 2 / 3 / 4 | 35 / 19 / 5 | 57 / 39 / 19 |
| `test_depth` | 5 / 6 | 2 / 1 | 13 / 7 |

At 25 families the model is far better on its own families than on anything else: the Study 01 gap,
reproduced on a new environment. At 1,600 families a held-out composition is solved about as often as a
practised family of the same depth (57 against 62-66 at two hops, 39 against 41-42 at three, 19 against 17-29
at four). **The gap between practised and unpractised structure is essentially closed; what limits the 0.5B
student is that it fails long chains wherever they come from.** The 1.5B student shows the same closing
(`test_comp` 82 / 84 / 66 against `test_id` 86 / 83 / 62 at D = 1,600) from a much higher base, and still loses
ground at four hops.

Other observations:

- **In-distribution accuracy falls with diversity for the small model** (own families 62.5% to 51.4%): with the
  episode budget fixed, 1,600 families get two examples each and the 0.5B student no longer masters them. The
  1.5B student does (89.3% to 86.9%). Whether more episodes would lift the 0.5B ceiling was not tested;
  Experiment 1 fixed the budget by design.
- **Behaviour improves across the board with D**: tool-selection F1 69% to 92%, dependency-unsafe episodes
  (an identifier used before it was observed, or a call made before its inputs existed) 36% to 9%, invented
  tools 1.06 to 0.16 per episode, unnecessary calls 2.4 to 0.8.
- **Reserved phrasings cost about 2 points** (36.4% against 38.8% at D = 1,600): wording is not what is hard.
- **Three of the 600 `test_comp` families were never solved** by any of the 26 trained runs in 52 attempts
  each; all three are four-hop chains through a list. The other 597 were each solved at least once, which is a
  late check on the tasks that the reference-model gate could not provide.
- **Replicates differ more than the plan assumed**: the standard deviation of replicate means on `test_comp`
  reaches 0.056 for the 0.5B student (at 400 families, where one replicate scored 42.2%) and 0.022 for the 1.5B
  student. The plan's threshold for using 8 seeds per arm in Experiment 2 is 0.03.

### 5.5 Deviations that affect what is reported

All are logged in the plan with the time they were decided.

- **Baselines** were scored on `dev`, `test_id` and `test_comp` only. They are at or near zero on all three.
- **The 1.5B student** was scored on its own families, `test_comp`, `test_id` and the v1 anchor; `dev`,
  `test_depth`, `test_tool` and `test_domain` are missing for it. It trained with gradient checkpointing and
  was scored 32 episodes at a time.
- **Reference-model gate**: shallow families only (§4.3).
- Both cuts were made to stay under the spending cap after scoring turned out three times as expensive as
  estimated, and before any `test_comp` result of a trained run had been looked at. All twenty 0.5B runs, which
  carry the confirmatory test, were scored on every split.

  minutes), because each turn of each episode re-reads the whole conversation and deep tasks make long
  conversations.
- **Failures.** Six of the 51 jobs ended in error: four ran out of GPU memory during scoring and two reached
  their time limit a few minutes before finishing. They were billed 5.08 USD together. Only the two stopped
  runs of the reference-model gate (0.62 USD) produced nothing that was used; the others had already saved
  their adapters or partial scores, and later jobs resumed from them. The out-of-memory failures came from a
  bug in the fallback that halves a batch, found and fixed during the run (§5.5 and the plan's deviations).

Per run, GPU-seconds on an A10G: 0.5B student 970 s training and 2,500-4,400 s scoring; 1.5B student about
2,900 s training (with gradient checkpointing) and 3,900 s scoring four splits.

## 7. Limitations

1. **The number of families is confounded with tool and edge coverage by design.** The like-for-like analysis
   that separates them is post-hoc. It indicates that both matter.
2. **The 0.5B ceiling sits on the decision threshold.** 37.7% with an interval of 33.7-41.5%. The point estimate
   is below 40% and I apply the rule as written, but the data do not exclude 40%.
3. **The 1.5B evidence is thinner**: two levels, three replicates, four of the eight splits.
4. **Episodes were fixed at 3,200.** The fall in own-family accuracy with D shows that the 0.5B student is short
   of either capacity or examples at high diversity, and this experiment cannot say which.
5. **The quality gate vouches for shallow families only.** Deeper test families are built from the same phrases
   and world-building code, the oracle solves all of them, and 597 of 600 `test_comp` families were solved by
   some student, but no independent model confirmed them before students were scored.
6. **Everything is one synthetic environment.** The v1 anchor shows limited transfer to hand-written tasks
   (20% for the 0.5B student). "The gap closes" is a statement about structure inside this generator's task
   space, not about tool use in general.
7. **Worlds contain references that lead nowhere.** Fields the gold path does not follow point at records that
   do not exist, so a wrong turn ends in "not found" more often than it would in a real system.
8. **`test_depth` is near the floor** for the 0.5B student and was not scored for the 1.5B student.
9. **Scoring noise was measured in Experiment 0 only** (0 of 720 episodes), with a batch of 64; Experiment 1
   used batches of 128 and 32 and a different decoding loop that was tested to give the same outputs.
10. **Labels are oracle trajectories.** Experiment 1 says nothing about teacher demonstrations or self-practice.
11. **The explanation of Study 01's instability (numerical precision under MLX) is a hypothesis.** The
    observations are that the same batches give the same loss and a 14-times smaller initial gradient norm
    here, and that the configuration which diverged there is stable here.

## 8. Recommendation on Experiment 2

### 8.1 What the pre-registered rules say

| rule | status |
|---|---|
| H1 supported for 0.5B **and** `test_comp` at D = 1,600 at least 40% -> Experiment 2 on the 0.5B student | first half met, second half not (37.7%) |
| H1 refuted or inconclusive for 0.5B, supported for 1.5B -> Experiment 2 on the 1.5B student | does not apply: H1 is supported for 0.5B |
| H1 supported but `test_comp` at D = 1,600 below 40% **for both sizes** -> revise the held-out definition or the student | does not apply: the 1.5B student is at 76.9% |
| H1 refuted for both sizes -> stop the loop programme | does not apply |
| held-out success at D = 1,600 below 40% for the chosen student -> simplify the held-out definition or raise model size (proposal §11) | applies to the 0.5B student |
| seed sd on `test_comp` above 0.03 -> 8 seeds per main arm | met for the 0.5B student (0.056); 0.022 for the 1.5B student, from three replicates |

No row of the experiment plan's table covers the outcome that occurred: H1 supported at both sizes with the
small student just under the ceiling threshold and the larger one far above it. The proposal's own stop/go
rule covers it, and points to raising the model size. That is a reading of the rules, so I flag it as a
judgment for you rather than an automatic consequence.

### 8.2 Recommendation

**Run Experiment 2 on `Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct`.** The case:

- The premise of Experiment 2 holds on this environment. Diversity of practised structure is a strong lever
  (+19 and +27 points), so a loop that widens what the student practises has something to gain.
- The starting position Experiment 2 assumes exists. A 1.5B student trained on 25 families has 89% on its own
  families and 50% on unpractised compositions: a narrow start with a 39-point gap, as in Study 01.
- There is room to measure the arms. The oracle ceiling at 1,600 families is 77%, 27 points above the
  25-family start, against a 10-point criterion for H2 and a 7.5-point non-inferiority margin.
- The 0.5B student would compress every arm into the 19-38% range, where a four-hop task is rarely solved
  whatever the training signal. A practice loop on it would mostly be measuring the depth limit.

**Do two things first.**

1. **Make rollouts cheaper, then re-cost Experiment 2.** Scoring was three times the estimate, and a practice
   arm is mostly rollouts (four sampled attempts per task, thousands of tasks per cycle, on a model three times
   the size). At the throughput measured here the proposal's 110 USD for Experiment 2 is too low by a factor I
   would put at three to five. Reusing the key-value cache between the turns of an episode, or serving the
   student with vLLM as the implementation plan already allows, should recover most of that. I recommend
   building and measuring it before asking for an Experiment 2 budget.
2. **Settle three design points in the Experiment 2 pre-registration**, each raised by these results:
   - the teacher. `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` solved 48% of one- and two-hop test families and 3% of four-hop ones
     with the few-shot prompt. The proposal already restricts the teacher's seed to 50 families of one or two
     hops, where that is workable, and falls back to oracle seeds below 80% acceptance; the static `teacher`
     arm over the whole practice space needs a stronger model than this or it will mostly measure the teacher;
   - seeds. Plan for 8 per main arm unless a 1.5B estimate from more than three replicates says otherwise;
   - depth. Report every endpoint by number of hops, and consider whether the curriculum should be allowed
     to weight by depth, since depth is where the remaining failures are.

**What I would not conclude.** That 0.5B models cannot do this: with oracle labels the small student closes
the composition gap as the larger one does, and nothing here tested more episodes, a different representation
of the task, or a different small model. And that self-practice will work: Experiment 1 used perfect labels
throughout and is silent on H2-H4.

### 8.3 Decisions for you

1. Approve Experiment 2 on the 1.5B student in principle, or keep the 0.5B student and accept a compressed
   range.
2. Approve the rollout-efficiency work and a re-costing before any Experiment 2 budget is set.
3. The teacher question of the proposal (§13) is still open and now sharper.
