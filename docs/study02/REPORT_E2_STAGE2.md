# Study 02, Experiment 2: Stage 2, the pilot

*2026-11-11. Stage 2 of the [Experiment 2 plan](../../experiments/study02_e2_signal/PLAN.md): replicate 0,
cycles 1 and 2 of the `practice` arm, run under the owner's approval of that date (cap 10 USD), with one
calibration of the corrected engine revision. Tables are generated by `scripts/s2_e2_stage2.py` into
[`results/study02/e2/stage2.md`](../../results/study02/e2/stage2.md). Stage 3 is not approved, nothing is
queued, and no sealed split was scored.*

## 1. Summary

| | result | gate |
|---|---|---|
| Yield | 34.2% in cycle 1, 45.2% in cycle 2 | met (20% in both) |
| `dev` | 25.7% (seed) → 35.8% → 47.0% | met (no fall over 5 points) |
| Cost per cycle | 0.94 and 1.00 of the cost model | met (within twice) |
| Verifier audit | **5.7% false accepts** | **not met** (2%) |
| Engine revision R6, corrected | outcomes reproduced; 0.43 s per episode | speed criterion (0.36 s) not met |
| Spend | **2.00 USD** of the 10 USD cap, three jobs, none failed | |

**The pilot did not pass, and the reason matters more than the gate.** Practice works: in two cycles the
student goes from 25.7% to 47.0% on held-out `dev` with no demonstration beyond the seed, and it becomes able
to solve three- and four-hop tasks it could not solve before. But a growing part of that is a shortcut the
simulated worlds allow and the validator cannot see. It was 1.9% of accepted episodes in cycle 1 and 9.4% in
cycle 2, and it reaches the evaluation: 43 of the cycle-2 adapter's 282 `dev` successes use it. Adapters
trained on oracle episodes almost never do (1 of 705 successes). **Run as designed, Stage 3 would compare
practice with oracle and teacher arms on an endpoint that favours practice.**

**Recommendation** (§6): do not run Stage 3 as designed. Amend the acceptance rule and the endpoint so that
neither counts an episode reached by the shortcut, record the amendment before any sealed split is scored,
and repeat the two-cycle pilot under it (about 2 USD). Then decide the main run.

## 4. The shortcut

**What it is.** In the generated worlds only the records on the gold path are fully built. A record that is
merely a candidate in a list refers to records that do not exist, and has nothing listed under it. A request
such as "the instrument used for the assay with the latest run date among those of doctor D" can then be
answered without finding the latest run date: ask for each listed assay's instrument until one exists. The
validator accepts the episode, because the right record was reached and the right fact stated.

Stage 1 found the first form, through "not found" errors, in 1 of 133 accepted episodes. The audit's reading
found a second form, through empty lists. `ccl.loop.by_elimination` recognises the first and was the
recorded measure for the gate; `ccl.loop.by_empty_list` recognises the second, was written after the reading
and is post-hoc.

**How much.**

| | cycle 1 | cycle 2 |
|---|---|---|
| accepted episodes | 480 | 480 |
| reached by "not found" | 3 (0.6%) | 29 (6.0%) |
| reached through empty lists (post-hoc) | 6 | 17 |
| **either** | **9 (1.9%)** | **45 (9.4%)** |
| of the accepted three-hop episodes | 3 of 54 | 24 of 106 |
| of the accepted four-hop episodes | 6 of 16 | 15 of 46 |
| accepted but not strictly successful | 4.0% | 12.3% |

In each of the 54 cases no other attempt at the task passed. The shortcut sits where the student is weakest:
a third of cycle 2's accepted four-hop episodes. The share of loose episodes rose by 8.3 points between the
cycles; the plan's warning stops a run above 10.

**The climb is real without it, and smaller.**

| hops | cycle 1 yield without the shortcut | cycle 2 | change, 95% interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 13.0% (51 of 393) | 27.6% (82 of 297) | +14.6 [+8.6, +20.7] |
| 4 | 2.4% (10 of 411) | 9.9% (31 of 312) | +7.5 [+3.9, +11.1] |

**It reaches the evaluation.** `dev` is scored by the same validator on worlds built the same way.

| adapter | `dev` success | without shortcut successes | at four hops |
|---|---|---|---|
| seed | 25.7% | 25.3% (2 successes) | 1.2% → 0.0% |
| after cycle 1 | 35.8% | 34.0% (11) | 5.4% → 1.2% |
| after cycle 2 | 47.0% | 39.8% (43) | 16.1% → 4.2% |

Two cycles of practice are worth 21 points on `dev` as scored and 14.5 points without the shortcut. At four
hops nearly all of the measured gain is the shortcut. For comparison, the two Experiment 1 adapters whose
traces Stage 1 produced, both trained on oracle episodes, use it in 0 of 350 and 1 of 355 successes. The
teachers use it in 10 of 122 and 11 of 155 accepted demonstrations.

So the bias has a direction. `practice` learns the shortcut from its own accepted episodes; `oracle` cannot,
because the oracle never takes it; `seed` barely does. Every confirmatory contrast of the plan compares a
practice arm with one of those on validator success. Experiment 1 is not affected in the same way: all its
arms were trained on oracle episodes.

## 5. The audit, and the engine

**Audit** (plan §5). 200 of the 960 accepted episodes, including all 78 that are not strictly successful.
80 differ from the oracle's solution and were read; verdicts are in
`results/study02/runs/e2/r0_practice/audit_verdicts.json`.

| verdict by reading | episodes |
|---|---|
| reached by trying candidates, "not found" | 32 |
| reached by trying candidates, empty lists | 21 |
| states a fact for the wrong record | 1 |
| no fault: an unnecessary, repeated or mistaken call, or the backup tool used at once | 26 |

Weighted back to the population, 5.6% are false accepts by reading, and 5.7% by the count recorded before
the pilot (elimination by "not found" or an unobserved identifier, whatever the reading). Both are over 2%.
The one wrong-record episode shows a second, unrelated weakness of the validator: for a two-part question
whose two answers are the same word, it checks that the word occurs, not that it is given for each part. One
episode invented an identifier; that call failed, and the record was then found properly.

**Engine revision R6, corrected.** The one calibration approved for this stage: 0.434 and 0.436 s per
episode; 4 and 1 of 400 outcomes changed; success +0.5 and −0.25 points; the same text as full re-encoding
on all 48 checked tasks. The diagnosis of its first failure was right, and the corrected version reproduces
Experiment 1's outcomes. It is 3.7 times faster than Experiment 1 and 1.5 times faster than the engine in
use, and it still misses the 0.36 s criterion, so by the rule it does not pass and stays off. It has the
same standing as the engine in use (outcome criteria met, speed criterion not), so adopting it is a decision,
not a consequence of a gate.

## 6. Recommendation for the next block

**Do not run Stage 3 as designed.** Its endpoint would favour the arms under test.

**Amend two things, in writing, before any sealed split is scored in Experiment 2:**

1. **Acceptance rule, for every data source.** An episode that `ccl.loop.shortcut` identifies (either form)
   is not admitted to a buffer. The check reads the trace only, never the gold graph, so the rule still
   uses only the outcome and runtime observations. It applies to practice, to the unverified control and to
   a teacher alike.
2. **Endpoint.** The primary endpoint becomes success without the shortcut: the validator passes and the
   trace shows no elimination. Validator success as it was is reported beside it for every arm. Experiment
   1's numbers can be restated under the same definition from its stored traces, at no cost.

**Then repeat the two-cycle pilot under the amended rule**, about 2 USD, cap 5 USD. It answers what this
pilot cannot: whether practice still climbs when the shortcut earns nothing, and whether the shortcut returns
in a third form. Stage 3 is decided after that.

The alternative is to remove the cause: rebuild the worlds so that every candidate record is complete. That
is the cleaner fix and the larger one. It changes `data/v2`, which is frozen, and with it every hash,
the quality gate and the comparison with Experiment 1; the 1.5B reference runs would have to be repeated
(about 10 USD) and the generator change reviewed. I recommend the amendment now and the rebuild only if the
repeated pilot shows the shortcut escaping the trace check.

**Decisions for you.**

| | decision | my recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | how to close the shortcut | amend the acceptance rule and the endpoint as above; rebuild the worlds only if that proves insufficient |
| 2 | repeat the pilot under the amended rule, cap 5 USD | approve |
| 3 | which engine | use the corrected R6 for the repeated pilot's rollouts and `dev`: it met the same two outcome criteria as the engine in use and is 1.5 times faster. That also measures it on sampled rollouts |
| 4 | sealed splits on the new engine | allow: the outcome criteria protect the evaluation and both engines meet them; this saves about 38 USD in Stages 2-3 |
| 5 | the validator's same-word weakness for two-part questions | measure how many test tasks it can affect before deciding; one case in 960 here |

**Budget.** With the pilot's two cycles as inputs the remaining main run costs 137 USD for block 3A and 69 USD
for 3B by the plan's rules, or 112 and 56 USD with sealed splits on the new engine. Adopting R6 lowers the
rollout and `dev` parts by about a third; that is not yet costed from a measurement of sampled rollouts.

## 7. Limits

- One replicate and two cycles. The intervals on the change in yield treat tasks as independent.
- The shortcut is recognised by two trace patterns, both written after seeing cases. A third form could
  exist and would not be counted here.
- "Without the shortcut" counts an episode as a failure if it used the shortcut at all. Some of those tasks
  the adapter might have solved properly on another attempt; under an amended rule it would have had to.
- `dev` without the shortcut was computed for three practice-arm adapters and, for oracle-trained adapters,
  on own-family tasks, not on `dev`: the 1.5B `dev` scoring of Experiment 1 is still on hold.
- No hypothesis of Experiment 2 has been tested.
