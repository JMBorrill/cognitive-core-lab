## 3. Stage 0: what was built

| | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| engine | `ccl/engine.py` | R1 weights in bfloat16 after merging; R2 each episode's key-value cache kept between turns; R3 groups sized by tokens; R4 a practice attempt stops at its first disqualifying error; sampling seeded per task, attempt and turn |
| loop | `ccl/loop.py` | seed set, one balanced task stream per replicate, acceptance, replay, curriculum buckets and weights, a resumable practice cycle, the structural-diversity table |
| jobs | `scripts/s2_e2.py` | the commands a Stage 1 job runs, and the CPU replay |
| money | `scripts/s2_launch.py` | a budget and a ledger per stage; the old budget closed |
| analysis | `scripts/s2_e2_stage1.py`, `scripts/s2_e2_rebudget.py` | this report's tables; the re-costing |

How the engine stays exact: at every turn the canonical prompt is rendered as before and compared with the
tokens already in the cache. The common prefix is kept, anything beyond it is switched off in the attention
mask, and the rest is read at its canonical positions. The model therefore sees the token sequence that full
re-encoding would give it.

**Gates of Stage 0, all met.** 73 tests pass on CPU and one is skipped (14 are new):

- cached decoding equals full re-encoding token for token, in groups of mixed length, with episodes ending
  at different turns and with text that does not survive re-tokenising;
- with the real Qwen tokenizer and the adapter of `e1_s05_D1600_r0` on CPU: 16 of 16 episodes give the same
  text as the Experiment 1 policy, and the committed outcomes;
- a practice cycle is deterministic, resumes from its attempt files to the same buffer, and stops at its
  targets; a stopped attempt is never accepted; a replay must reproduce the recorded observations;
- no sealed input is reachable from the loop, the engine or the job script; the stream holds no held-out
  family or edge;
- every job spec passes a dry run, and all of them together fit under the cap even if each ran to its time
  limit.

Every Stage 1 command was also run end to end on CPU at toy scale before any paid job. That found two
faults that would otherwise have cost a job.

## 4. Stage 1: what was measured

### 4.1 Engine (a)

Own-family tasks of two Experiment 1 adapters, scored again and compared with the committed rows.

| adapter | s per episode | Experiment 1 | outcomes changed | success | Experiment 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e1_s15_D1600_r0` | 0.658 | 1.62 | 4 of 400 (1.0%) | 87.5% | 87.5% |
| `e1_s15_D25_r0` | 0.649 | 1.62 | 1 of 400 (0.25%) | 88.75% | 89.0% |

The engine is trustworthy and less fast than planned. It reads 970 tokens per episode where Experiment 1
read 2,600, yet a decoding step takes 0.18 s for a group of about 100 episodes. The cause is in how the
library computes masked attention: with 12 query heads on 2 key-value heads, it copies every layer's cache
out to 12 heads at every step.

**One revision was tried and failed** (plan, deviation 1). R6 computed that attention per key-value head. It
ran at 0.29 s per episode and was wrong: 113 of 400 outcomes changed. A guard written for this case stopped
the job after four minutes, before anything was built on it. The fault is numerical: R6 rounded attention
scores to bfloat16 before the softmax, where the library's GPU kernel keeps them in float32. On CPU both
paths round, so the CPU checks could not see it. R6 was recorded beforehand as a single revision, so it is
switched off and was not tried again. The numerics are corrected in the code but **have not been run on a
GPU**. Everything below uses the engine as first calibrated.

### 4.2 Seed adapter and training cost (c)

The seed adapter of replicate 0 (400 oracle episodes, 50 families of one or two hops, 100 steps) scores
**25.7%** on `dev`: 85%, 51%, 4% and 1% at one to four hops.

The plan asks that this be at least 15 points below the 1,600-family adapters' `dev` score. Those scores
belong to the 1.5B scoring Experiment 1 left out, which was approved for planning only, so they do not
exist. An estimate stands in for them: for the 0.5B student `dev` lies between `test_comp` and `test_id` at
both levels of D, and for the 1.5B student those two are 76.9% and 81.4%. The seed is then 51 to 56 points
below. The margin is large enough that the estimate's error does not matter, but it is an estimate. Replacing
it by a measurement costs 0.50 USD (§7).

Training on a cycle-1 buffer (the stream's mix of one to four hops) takes **3.60 s per step** with gradient
checkpointing always on, as in Experiment 1. With checkpointing only above 6,000 tokens, and without it,
training runs out of memory. The 10 to 15 USD the plan hoped to save there is not available.

### 4.3 Yield probe (c)

The first 400 tasks of replicate 0's stream, four sampled attempts each, 0.364 s per attempt.

| hops | tasks | accepted | yield | one attempt passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 71 | 58 | 82% | 72% |
| 2 | 101 | 52 | 52% | 36% |
| 3 | 112 | 19 | 17% | 6% |
| 4 | 116 | 4 | 3% | 1% |
| **all** | **400** | **133** | **33.3%** | **24%** |

By number of list selections the fall is steeper: 82%, 44%, 19% for none, one and two; 2 accepted of 68
tasks with three or four. At this yield a cycle needs about 1,440 tasks for its 480 accepted episodes, inside
the cap of 2,400.

Of the 1,075 attempts that failed with no format error, 624 made fewer calls than the gold graph has: the
seed adapter mostly answers too early. Stopping at the first disqualifying error (R4) ended only 9% of
attempts.

### 4.4 Is the diversity real?

Three separate questions.

**The stream offers real structural variety.** Replicate 0's 14,400 tasks hold 4,366 of the 5,364 practice
families, every one of the 79 tools and 229 practised edges, and 14,381 distinct requests. A family recurs at
most nine times, once per pass, each time as a new instance. Experiment 1 already showed that this kind of
variety moves the endpoint (27 points for this student at fixed episodes), so it is not superficial.

**What practice accepts is broad but shallow.**

| | offered (400) | accepted by practice (133) | oracle arm, same number of tasks | seed set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| distinct families | 400 | 133 | 133 | 50 |
| tools covered, of 79 | 79 | 73 | 73 | 50 |
| edges covered, of 229 | 199 | 78 | 138 | 26 |
| share with three or four hops | 57% | 17% | 57% | 0% |
| list selections per task | 1.60 | 0.88 | 1.65 | - |
| family not in the seed | 95% | 90% | 93% | - |
| needs an edge the seed lacks | 80% | 52% | 78% | - |

Every accepted episode is a different family, 90% of them outside the seed, and half need an edge the seed
never showed. That is new structure, not more instances of what was demonstrated. But the selection is
strongly towards the easy end: the accepted set has half the list selections per task of the stream, covers
78 edges where the oracle arm covers 138 on as many tasks, and holds 23 episodes deeper than the seed.
Chained selection is what limits this student (review, §3.2), and it is what the accepted set lacks. This is
the selection effect the `oracle-matched` control was added to measure.

**The trajectories add no new behaviour.** 126 of the 133 accepted episodes make exactly the oracle's calls;
7 make more; none is shorter. Only 17 tasks have two different passing attempts. So a self-generated episode
is, on a task the student can solve, the oracle's episode without the label. The value of practice here is
that it needs no solver, and its cost is the selection above.

### 4.5 Verification

- **Replay.** All 382 admissible practice attempts, and all 277 accepted teacher demonstrations, were replayed
  on CPU in a separate process from their recorded turns. None failed.
- **Preliminary audit.** All 133 accepted practice episodes were compared programmatically with the oracle's
  calls and answer, and the 9 that differ were read. Seven make an unnecessary call; two print a number as
  `87.8` for `87.80`. None states a wrong fact. The formal audit of 200 episodes is the pilot's.
- **A loophole in the simulated worlds, found by reading.** A record that is not on the gold path refers to
  records that do not exist. An attempt can therefore skip the selection rule: it asks for the referenced
  record of one listed candidate after another until one is found. The validator accepts it, since the right
  record was reached and the right fact stated. `ccl.loop.by_elimination` now recognises this from the trace.

  | data source | accepted episodes found by elimination | strictly successful |
  |---|---|---|
  | practice, seed adapter | 1 of 133 (0.8%) | 126 of 133 (95%) |
  | `Qwen3-14B` demonstrations | 7 of 122 (5.7%) | 83 of 122 (68%) |
  | `Qwen3-32B` demonstrations | 8 of 155 (5.2%) | 120 of 155 (77%) |

  For practice this is inside the audit's 2% limit. It is a shortcut a learner could come to prefer, so it
  should be counted every cycle. The teachers use it six times as often. Experiment 1's students were trained
  on oracle episodes, which never do this; whether their test scores contain any is not known, because the
  check needs traces and those are in the storage bucket, not the repository.

### 4.6 Teacher qualification (d)

200 practice tasks, 50 per number of hops, few-shot prompt, one greedy attempt and one retry sampled at
temperature 0.7.

| model | accepted | 1 hop | 2 | 3 | 4 | qualifies | USD per accepted demonstration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Qwen/Qwen3-14B` | 61.0% | 86% | 74% | 54% | 30% | no: under 40% at four hops | 0.0040 |
| `Qwen/Qwen3-32B` | 77.5% | 96% | 90% | 66% | 58% | **yes** | 0.0071 |

`Qwen3-32B` also meets both thresholds without its eight demonstrations found by elimination (73.5% and
46%). Counting only strictly successful demonstrations it would not (60% and 20%); that is not the rule, and
it is reported because a third of what this teacher would hand the student contains an unnecessary or
unsafe call. The sampled retry adds little: 6 of 51 tasks.

Demonstrations for five replicates would cost about 102 USD, above the plan's 60 USD line, so the plan's rule
gives a teacher arm of three replicates at about 61 USD. The mixture-of-experts 30B model named beforehand
was replaced by `Qwen3-32B` before any teacher job (deviation 3).

**Approve Stage 2 as the plan defines it** (replicate 0, cycles 1 and 2 of `practice`), at its cap of 10 USD.
Expected cost about 3 USD; the probe's 400 tasks are already paid for and the pilot continues from them.
Inside that cap, add two small items:

   0.36 s is not known. If it fails, nothing else changes. It is one attempt, announced here, not open-ended

2. **`dev` for the three 1,600-family adapters**, 0.50 USD, so that criterion (c) and the pilot's monitoring
   rest on a measurement. This is part of the deferred scoring and needs your approval as such. The rest of
   that scoring (3.29 USD more) can wait for Stage 3, where every split is reported.

**Do not approve Stage 3 yet.** Its cost is now known to within the scenarios above, but its point is not:

- If cycle 2's adapter is accepted on clearly more three- and four-hop tasks than the seed adapter (17% and

## 9. Deviations and limits

Recorded in the plan before the jobs they concern: (1) one engine revision, R6, tried and failed;
(2) gradient checkpointing stays always on; (3) `Qwen3-32B` replaces the 30B mixture-of-experts model as
second teacher candidate.

Limits of what Stage 1 shows:

- One replicate, one seed adapter, the first 400 tasks. The yield has a 95% interval of about 29% to 38%.
- Nothing is known about cycles after the first.
- The seed-headroom criterion rests on an estimate.
- The engine was calibrated on greedy decoding of own-family tasks. Sampled decoding shares the code path
  but has no reference to be compared with; the replay covers its accepted outputs only.
- The elimination check is a reading of traces. It was written after seeing one case, and it finds cases of
  that shape.
- No sealed split was scored, and no hypothesis of Experiment 2 has been tested.
