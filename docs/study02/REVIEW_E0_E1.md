## 1. Verdict

**The PR meets the scientific and engineering gates, and I recommend merging it, together with the
follow-up commits of this review.** The confirmatory result stands under every check made here: H1 is
supported for the 0.5B student, +18.9 points on `test_comp` between 25 and 1,600 training families. The
recommendation to run Experiment 2 on the 1.5B student follows from the proposal's stop/go rule as the
report says, and the report is right to call it a reading of the rules.

Nothing found changes a conclusion. Six things needed correcting or qualifying (§4). One was a defect
in code that matters for the next experiment: the launcher reserved one billing minute too few for a job
that hits its time limit.

What was not verified: the one test that needs the real model weights (parity of the first training step
with the Study 01 log) was skipped, because the model hub is not reachable from this session; and no
adapter was re-scored, because that needs a GPU. Adapters and traces live in the storage bucket and were
not inspected. The mixed-model file (`glmm.json`) was not regenerated: it needs `statsmodels`, which is not in
the lock file.

## 3. The four points raised for this review

### 3.1 Incomplete secondary scoring

What is missing, exactly ([review.md](../../results/study02/review/review.md), A3): the six 1.5B adapters
have no `dev`, `test_depth`, `test_tool` or `test_domain` rows; the four untrained baselines have only
`dev`, `test_id` and `test_comp`. Every other cell is a complete split. No partial split entered a table.

- **Effect on the conclusions: none on H1.** The 1.5B contrast is a secondary test and was scored in full
  on the primary endpoint.
- **Effect on what can be said about the 1.5B student:** "diversity closes the composition gap" rests, for
  that student, on `test_comp`, `test_id` and own families at two levels and three replicates. Nothing is
  known about its depth limit (`test_depth`) or about unseen tools and domains.
- **It matters for Experiment 2**, which monitors on `dev` and reports all splits. The six adapters are in
  the bucket; scoring the four missing splits costs about 8 USD and is the first item of Stage 1 in the
  [Experiment 2 plan](../../experiments/study02_e2_signal/PLAN.md). Those numbers will be labelled as
  completed after the fact.
- One inaccuracy: the Experiment 1 plan (deviation 5) says partial 1.5B `dev` rows exist. The committed
  files contain none.

### 3.2 Low four-hop performance of the reference model

The gate's reference model (`Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, few-shot) solved 83% of one-hop tasks, 35% of two-hop
tasks and 3% of four-hop tasks (B6). At four hops it stopped after three turns on average where five are
the minimum. I agree with the report that this is the model and not the tasks, for three reasons:

1. A trained 1.5B student solves 66% of four-hop `test_comp` tasks. Their failures are missing calls and
   early answers (B5), not a limit: no episode comes near the 256-token cap, and the eight-turn limit ends
   0.3% of its `test_comp` episodes.
2. **A new check re-derives every list selection from the task's world** (A7): the rule is read back from
   the request and must single out exactly one record, the one the gold graph then uses. 5,436 selections
   in the six evaluation files, 0 failures; 20,204 in a practice sample, 0 failures. Planted ties are caught
   in 300 of 300 trials on `test_comp` tasks and planted wrong picks in 233 of 244; a unit test keeps that so.
3. The report counted families never solved by any trained run (3 of 600). Counted by task it is 74 of
   1,200 (B3). I read the 26 of at most three hops with their oracle transcripts: all are well-formed.
   They are hard because they chain two to four selections ("the invoice with the latest due date among
   those on the account that made the payment with the lowest amount among...") or need a weekday turned
   into a date.

What remains true: **no independent model vouched for the three- to six-hop test families**, which are 70%
of the primary endpoint. The checks above cover consistency and uniqueness of the gold answer at every
depth. They do not cover whether a request reads naturally. The 48 never-solved four-hop tasks have not
been read; the Experiment 2 plan requires it before any sealed split is scored again.

A finding from that reading: **difficulty tracks the number of list selections in a request more closely
than the number of hops.** At 1,600 families the 0.5B student solves 82 / 51 / 25 / 14 / 6% of `test_comp`
tasks with 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 selections, and the 1.5B student 92 / 87 / 72 / 61 / 47%. "Depth is what
limits the 0.5B student" should be read as "chained selection is".

For Experiment 2 this model is not usable as a teacher beyond one hop, as the report says. The plan sets a
qualification gate with a four-hop floor.

### 3.3 Structural diversity controls

Verified: family identity is the gold graph; one fault variant per base graph; every nested subset has the
same mix of hops and modifiers (2.9 gold calls per task at every level); hold-outs are assigned by hash
and are clean. D is confounded with tool and edge coverage by construction, which the plan recorded before
the runs and the report states as its first limitation.

What the evidence supports, in order of strength:

- **Pre-registered:** more families help on `test_comp` (+18.9). Restricted to test families whose tools
  the run's training set contains, still +14.1 [9.6, 18.0]. Restricted to those whose practised edges it
  also contains, +4.1 [−2.4, 9.9]: inconclusive, and it compares different families at the two levels.
- **Post-hoc (the report's):** on the same test families, those a 25-family set already covers, 34% to 46%.
  Every replicate is positive (+7.5 to +17.7).

So "the effect is not only coverage" is a post-hoc finding with consistent replicates. The pre-registered
endpoint closest to it is inconclusive. The report says the first and should say the second beside it.

Two qualifications the report does not make:

- **`test_id` is not a set of practised families** (B1). It holds 200 families of the practice space; a
  run's training subset contains 1% of them at D = 25 and 57% at D = 1,600. Its rise with D (30.9% to
  50.9%) therefore mixes generalisation with a growing practised share. Where §5.4 of the report compares
  held-out compositions with "a practised family", only the own-family row is that.
- **At four hops the gap has not closed for the 0.5B student:** 29% on its own families, 19% on `test_comp`
  and 17% on `test_id`. At two and three hops it has (66 against 57, 42 against 39). The report gives these
  numbers; its sentence "essentially closed" needs "up to three hops".

For Experiment 2 the consequence is larger than for Experiment 1: arms that select their own training
tasks will differ in exactly these properties. The plan gives every arm of a replicate one balanced task
stream, measures each arm's realised diversity, and adds a control trained on oracle solutions for the
tasks practice selected.

### 3.4 Declining in-distribution accuracy

Own-family success of the 0.5B student is 62.5%, 52.5%, 50.0% and 51.4% at 25, 100, 400 and 1,600 families
(B2).

- **It is real.** Every replicate falls from 25 to 1,600 families: −11.1 points, Student-t interval
  [−14.3, −7.9]. It holds on identical tasks: on the 100 own-family tasks per replicate that the 25- and
  100-family runs were both given, −11.2 [−17.5, −4.9].
- **It is one step, not a trend.** All of it happens between 25 and 100 families (−9.9 [−11.9, −8.0]).
  From 100 to 1,600 it is flat (−1.1 [−5.1, +2.8]), while instances per family fall from 32 to 2.
- **It is the loss of heavy repetition, at depth.** At 25 families each family is seen 128 times. On
  identical tasks the fall is at three and four hops (63% to 37%, 46% to 31%); one-hop tasks improve.
- **The 1.5B student does not show it**: 89.3% to 86.9%, interval [−4.9, +0.1], and 92% against 91% on
  identical tasks.

The report's wording, "diversity costs the small model in-distribution accuracy", is accurate for 25
against 1,600 families and misleading as a description of the curve. Nothing gets worse beyond 100
families, and on every fixed set of tasks other than a run's own heavily repeated families, more families
is better. For Experiment 2 it is one more reason for the 1.5B student, which does not need the repetition.
