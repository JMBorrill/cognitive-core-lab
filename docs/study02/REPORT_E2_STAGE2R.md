**The pilot passed.** With shortcut episodes kept out of the buffers, practice still climbs: three-hop yield
goes from 12.8% to 27.0% and four-hop from 3.5% to 11.3%.

**The headline number is not the one to trust.** After two cycles the adapter scores:

| measure, cycle-2 adapter | |
|---|---|
| `dev`, original validator (comparable with everything before) | 44.2% |
| `dev`, each fact for its own part | 43.5% |
| `dev`, and no shortcut | **40.8%** |
| the 350 selection tasks of `dev`, original worlds | 23.7% |
| the same 350 tasks on complete worlds (adversarial set) | **18.3%** |

Protocol 2 cleaned the training data. It did not remove the behaviour: the cycle-2 adapter still shows the
shortcut in 35 of 600 `dev` episodes and owes 16 of its 265 successes to it, although no buffered episode
contains it. The original validator on the original worlds therefore still overstates, by 3.4 points here.
The shortcut-free measure and the adversarial set agree with each other (19.1% and 18.3% on the same 350
tasks), which is the evidence that either is a sound measure.

**Recommendation** (§6): the pilot's gates are met, so the next decision is Stage 3. Before approving it,
fix its endpoint as success without the shortcut, with the adversarial construction applied to the test tasks
as a second measure, and settle the teacher arm, which no model currently qualifies for under protocol 2.

## 3. What protocol 2 kept out

| cycle | tasks offered | accepted | admissible under protocol 1 | attempts showing the shortcut | attempts with a wrong part | in the buffer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,444 | 480 | 492 | 110 of 5,776 | 24 | 0 shortcut, 0 wrong part |
| 2 | 1,143 | 480 | 504 | 116 of 4,572 | 10 | 0 shortcut, 0 wrong part |

The rule cost 12 tasks in cycle 1 and 24 in cycle 2: tasks whose only passing attempts used the shortcut or
gave a fact for the wrong part. The first pilot had admitted 9 and 45 shortcut episodes in its two cycles.
The share of accepted episodes that are strictly successful is 98% in both cycles, where the first pilot fell
from 96% to 88%.

The shortcut is still attempted in rollouts, at about 2% of attempts in cycle 1 and 2.5% in cycle 2. Under
protocol 1 the count of accepted shortcut episodes grew fivefold between cycles; here the behaviour is not
rewarded and its rate in rollouts barely moves.

## 5. Evaluation, three ways

### 5.1 `dev`, unchanged

| adapter | original validator | each fact for its own part | and no shortcut | successes by shortcut | episodes showing it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed | 25.8% | 25.3% | 25.0% | 2 of 155 | 9 of 600 |
| after cycle 1 | 30.7% | 30.2% | 29.5% | 4 of 184 | 10 of 600 |
| after cycle 2 | 44.2% | 43.5% | 40.8% | 16 of 265 | 35 of 600 |

By depth, without the shortcut, the cycle-2 adapter is at 85%, 65%, 36% and 5% for one to four hops (seed:
85%, 50%, 4%, 0%). At four hops the original validator says 11.3%; less than half of that survives.

First pilot, for comparison, original validator: 25.7% → 35.8% → 47.0%; without shortcut successes: 25.3% →
34.0% → 39.8%. After two cycles the two pilots stand at the same place on the shortcut-free measure (39.8%
and 40.8%), and the first pilot's higher headline (47.0% against 44.2%) was the shortcut: 43 of its successes
against 16 here.

The per-part check changes little on `dev`: three or four successes per adapter state a fact for the wrong
part. It mattered more in rollouts (34 attempts rejected).

### 5.2 The adversarial set

350 `dev` tasks in which a call depends on a list selection, rebuilt so that every candidate's continuation
exists. Request, gold path and answer are those of the original, and an agent that applies the rule sees the
same episode on both.

| adapter | original tasks | original, no shortcut | **adversarial twins** | solved on the original only / on the twin only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed | 7.1% | 6.6% | 7.1% | 1 / 1 |
| after cycle 1 | 12.0% | 10.9% | 10.9% | 5 / 1 |
| after cycle 2 | 23.7% | 19.1% | 18.3% | 20 / 1 |

| cycle-2 adapter, by hops | original | original, no shortcut | adversarial |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (50 tasks) | 48.0% | 44.0% | 46.0% |
| 3 (136) | 29.4% | 27.2% | 24.3% |
| 4 (164) | 11.6% | 4.9% | 4.9% |

Three readings.

- **Practice improves real selection, not only the shortcut.** On complete worlds the adapter goes from 7.1% to
  18.3% in two cycles.
- **The incomplete worlds give away about five points on these tasks** (23.7% against 18.3%): 20 tasks solved
  on the original and not on its twin, against one the other way.
- **The trace check is a sound correction.** "Original, no shortcut" and the adversarial score agree at every
  adapter and nearly at every depth, although one is computed from traces on the original worlds and the
  other by changing the worlds.

## 6. Recommendation for the next block

The gates of the pilot are met under protocol 2, so the next decision is whether and how to run Stage 3.
**It is not approved, and I recommend settling four things first.** None costs compute.

| | decision | my recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stage 3's primary endpoint | **Success without the shortcut** (`success_clean`) on `test_comp`, with the original validator's success reported beside it for comparison with Experiment 1. The original measure favours arms that practise: 3.4 points here after two cycles, and more at depth. |
| 2 | adversarial test tasks | Build complete-world twins of the test tasks the same way (CPU only, deterministic, hashed), and score them as a second, pre-registered measure. It is the only measure that does not rest on a detector. |
| 3 | the teacher arm | Under protocol 2 no teacher qualifies on the Stage 1 data (`Qwen3-32B`: 67.5% overall, 30% at four hops; thresholds 60% and 40%). Either report H2(b) as not tested, as the plan provides, or approve a re-qualification under protocol 2 (about 1 to 2 USD). I would not lower the thresholds. |
| 4 | sealed splits on the corrected engine | Allow: it met both outcome criteria in its calibration. Stage 3A then costs about 91 USD instead of 121. |

**On rebuilding the worlds.** The audit passed, so the condition you set for that investigation is not
triggered. One fact argues for keeping it in view: the shortcut behaviour persists at evaluation without
being rewarded, and grew from 9 to 35 `dev` episodes over two cycles. It looks like a recovery habit ("that
did not exist, try the next one") that the incomplete worlds reward at test time even when training does
not. Decisions 1 and 2 make the measurement robust to it. Only complete training worlds would remove the
incentive, and that changes `data/v2` and the comparison with Experiment 1.

**Budget.** With this pilot's measurements the main run costs 121 USD for block 3A and 60 USD for 3B by the
plan's rules, or 91 and 44 USD with sealed splits on the corrected engine. Scoring an adversarial test set
would add to that and is not yet costed.

## 7. Limits

- One replicate, two cycles, one seed adapter. The first pilot and this one differ in engine, protocol and
  sampled attempts at once; where they agree (the shortcut-free climb, the shortcut-free `dev` score after two
  cycles) the agreement is informative, and where they differ (cycle 1's `dev` score, 35.8% against 30.7%)
  one replicate cannot say why.
- The shortcut detector describes the forms seen so far. The audit's wider flag found no other form in 200
  episodes; that bounds a third form, it does not exclude one.
- A candidate picked by luck and a candidate picked by the rule leave the same trace. Sampling four attempts
  per task admits some lucky picks into a buffer. They are correct episodes for their task, they are the same
  on complete worlds, and no check here can count them.
- The adversarial set covers the tasks where a call depends on a selection (350 of 600), to the depth of each
  task and for the tools of its gold path.
- The per-part check attributes facts by the wording of the answer. It is exact for answers in the trained
  format and a heuristic for free wording.
- No hypothesis of Experiment 2 has been tested.

## 8. Reproduce

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q                                            # 88 tests on CPU
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests/test_e2_protocol2.py                 # the amendment's tests
.venv/bin/python scripts/s2_e2.py replay results/study02/runs/e2/r0_practice_p2   # 2,951 attempts, protocol 2
.venv/bin/python scripts/s2_e2.py audit results/study02/runs/e2/r0_practice_p2    # the audit's sample
.venv/bin/python scripts/s2_e2_stage2r.py                                # results/study02/e2/stage2r.{json,md}
.venv/bin/python scripts/s2_launch.py status --budget e2_stage2r
```

Attempt traces of both cycles, both buffers, the arm's state, the audit and its verdicts are committed. The
adversarial set is not stored: `ccl.adversarial.adversarial_dev()` rebuilds it, and its SHA-256 is in
`configs/study02/e2/protocol2.json`.
