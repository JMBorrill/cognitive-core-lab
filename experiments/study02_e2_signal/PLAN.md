## 1. Question

Can a small model close its generalisation gap by practising in a verifiable environment: attempting
tasks, receiving only pass or fail from the executable validator, and retraining on its own verified
successes? And how does that compare, at the same training budget, with being shown solutions?

What Experiment 1 established for the student used here, `Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct`, trained on oracle labels:

| | 25 training families | 1,600 training families |
|---|---|---|
| `test_comp` (primary endpoint) | 50.0% | 76.9% |
| own training families | 89.3% | 86.9% |

So the lever exists (+26.9 points from structural diversity alone), there is room above a narrow start,
and 77% is the reference for what perfect labels and broad practice give on this task space.

## 2. Hypotheses

As pre-registered in the proposal (§3). Success is the validator's; the endpoint is `test_comp`.

| | hypothesis | supported if | refuted if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H2 autonomy** (primary) | `practice+curriculum` closes the gap without further demonstrations | (a) it gains ≥ 10 points over `seed`, interval above zero, **and** (b) it is non-inferior to `teacher` at a margin of 7.5 points | (a) fails, or the interval of (`practice+curriculum` − `teacher`) lies entirely below −7.5 |
| **H3 curriculum** | weakness-targeted selection beats uniform selection | `practice+curriculum` − `practice` ≥ 5 points, interval above zero | interval entirely below +5 |
| **H4 verification** | the verifier is what makes practice work | `practice` − `practice-unverified` ≥ 5 points, interval above zero | interval entirely below +5 |

Anything else is inconclusive. Two points are settled here that the proposal left open:

- **If no teacher qualifies** (§7, Stage 1), H2(b) is reported as not tested. The programme decision then
  rests on the criterion the proposal already names in its stop/go table: the share of the oracle's gain
  that practice achieves, (`practice+curriculum` − `seed`) / (`oracle` − `seed`), with its interval.
- **H2(b) is tested only if H2(a) holds.** If practice does not gain 10 points over the seed, H2 is refuted
  whatever a teacher achieves, and the teacher arm (the most expensive and least certain line of the
  budget) is not run.

My prior for H3 remains below even, and at five replicates the design detects a difference of 5.6 to 7.2
points with 80% power ([budget.md](budget.md)). H3 will probably end inconclusive unless the effect is
larger than its threshold. It stays in because the curriculum arm is the one H2 is defined on.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Student and training settings

`Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` at revision `989aa7980e4c`, with the settings Experiment 0 fixed and
Experiment 1 used for this model: LoRA rank 16, alpha 32, all blocks, bias-corrected AdamW, lr 1e-4, 20
warm-up steps, cosine decay to 10%, gradient clipping 1.0, 8 episodes per step, length limit 4,096,
gradient checkpointing. No setting is tuned in this experiment.

### 3.2 Replicates

Replicate *r* fixes three things that every arm of that replicate shares: the seed set, the task stream
and the training seed. Arms are therefore compared within a replicate on the same starting adapter and
the same candidate tasks, and the statistics pair on replicate, family and task.

- **Seed set.** 400 oracle episodes: 8 training instances of each of 50 practice families of one or two
  hops, drawn with the balanced sampler of Experiment 1 (`ccl.compose.balanced_order`, seed
  `e2|seed|r`). The proposal's seed was teacher demonstrations with an oracle fallback below 80%
  acceptance; the only teacher measured so far solves 35% of two-hop tasks, so the fallback is taken now.
  No arm other than `teacher` then depends on a teacher model.
- **Seed adapter.** Trained from the base model on the seed set: 100 steps, two passes.

### 3.3 Task stream: the structural-diversity control

Experiment 1 showed that which families a training set contains moves the endpoint by 27 points. In the
proposal's design the arms drew tasks independently and uniformly over practice families, 54% of which have
four hops. Two arms could then differ in depth and family mix by chance, and none would be comparable with
the Experiment 1 reference. Instead:

- Replicate *r* has **one stream** of practice tasks, built before any arm runs: nine passes of 1,600
  tasks, each pass a fresh balanced draw of 1,600 families (`e2|stream|r|pass`) with a new instance of
  each. Every pass has the composition of an Experiment 1 training subset: 18 / 25 / 28 / 29% for one to
  four hops and the same modifier mix. 14,400 tasks is what a practice arm can attempt in six cycles.
- **Every arm reads that stream from the start, in order.** `oracle` keeps the first 480 tasks of each
  cycle. `practice` and `teacher` go through as many as they need to have 480 accepted. They see the same
  tasks in the same order and differ in which ones they can solve.
- What an arm ends up training on is an outcome of the arm and is **measured, per arm, cycle and
  replicate**: distinct families and base graphs, hops, modifiers, list selections per task, tool coverage
  (of 79), edge coverage (of 229), gold calls per task. This table is reported beside every result.
- The `oracle-matched` control separates *which tasks* from *whose trajectories* (§3.4).

Every generated task passes the selection check of the review (`scripts/s2_review.py`, A7) before it is
offered to any arm; a task that fails is dropped and logged.

### 3.4 Arms

Each adds 2,880 accepted episodes to the seed set in 6 cycles of 480.

| arm | replicates | where a cycle's 480 episodes come from | what the learner is told |
|---|---|---|---|
| `seed` (control) | 0-4 | nothing is added | — |
| `oracle` | 0-4 | oracle trajectories on the next 480 stream tasks | full solutions from a privileged solver |
| `practice` | 0-4 | the current adapter's own attempts on stream tasks, 4 sampled attempts each | pass or fail |
| `practice+curriculum` | 0-4 | as `practice`, with tasks drawn towards measured weakness (§3.6) | pass or fail |
| `practice-unverified` (control) | 0-2 | as `practice`, but one well-formed attempt per task is kept at random | nothing |
| `oracle-matched` (control, new) | 0-2 | oracle trajectories on exactly the tasks in that replicate's final `practice+curriculum` buffer | full solutions, on the tasks practice selected |
| `teacher` (Stage 4, conditional) | 0-4 | a qualified open-weight teacher: one greedy attempt and one sampled retry per stream task | full solutions from a larger model |

`oracle-matched` needs no rollouts. `practice+curriculum` against it shows what self-generated trajectories
cost on identical tasks; it against `oracle` shows what the tasks that practice could solve cost against
the tasks it was offered.

### 3.5 One cycle of a practice arm

1. Take tasks from the stream (in order, or by the curriculum).
2. The current adapter attempts each task four times at temperature 0.8 (top-p 0.95), without the few-shot
   prompt. An attempt stops at its first parse error, unknown tool or invalid argument, since the
   acceptance rule can no longer admit it.
3. The validator returns pass or fail. The learner never sees the gold graph.
4. The common acceptance rule of the proposal (§6) applies: the episode passes the validator with no
   parse error, unknown tool or invalid argument; of several passing attempts the one with the fewest
   calls is kept, the earliest on a tie. For `practice-unverified`, "well-formed" is that rule without the
   validator: a final answer, and no parse error, unknown tool or invalid argument.
5. The cycle ends at 480 accepted episodes or after 2,400 tasks.
6. Every accepted episode is replayed on CPU from its recorded calls against a fresh simulator and must
   pass the validator again before it enters the buffer (§5).
7. A new adapter is trained from the base model on seed plus buffer, two passes, and scored on `dev`.

### 3.6 Curriculum

Practice tasks are grouped by number of hops (1-4), shape (plain chain, with a list selection, ending in an
action, fan-out, twin) and modifier class (none, fault, stop). Groups with fewer than 30 practice families
are merged with their nearest neighbour in hops; the resulting list of buckets is frozen with the other
inputs. A bucket's weight is `p(1 − p) + 0.05`, where `p` is the share of attempted tasks accepted in that
bucket over the last two cycles, with one pseudo-task at 0.5 so that an untried bucket starts at 0.5. A fifth
of each cycle's tasks is taken in stream order regardless. Within a bucket, tasks are taken in stream
order. The curriculum reads practice outcomes only: not `dev`, not any test split.

### 3.7 Matched training budgets

- **Steps and episodes.** Every arm's final adapter is trained from the base model on seed plus buffer
  with the same settings: 3,280 episodes, 820 steps of 8 episodes, two passes. The adapter after cycle
  *k* takes (400 + 480*k*) / 4 steps. Arms differ only in which episodes they hold.
- **`seed` is the exception by definition**: 400 episodes, 100 steps. That more optimisation alone does
  not explain a gain over it is what `practice-unverified` controls for.
- **Shortfall.** If a cycle reaches its task cap with fewer than 480 accepted episodes, the arm carries on
  with what it has and its final adapter still takes 820 steps. The primary analysis uses every arm as it
  ended (the shortfall is part of what autonomy costs). If any replicate of a practice arm ends more than
  5% short, the `oracle` adapter of that replicate is also trained on its first *n* episodes, *n* being
  what the practice arm reached, and that contrast is reported as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Static arms** train two adapters, after cycles 3 and 6. Their intermediate adapters are not needed to
  generate data, and two points are enough to compare arms at equal episodes mid-run.
- **Reported per arm and never hidden inside the match:** GPU-seconds of rollouts including rejected
  attempts, attempts made, oracle calls, teacher tokens, supervised tokens trained on.

## 4. Cheaper rollouts

Experiment 1 measured 1.4 to 1.6 s per scored episode for this student and about 12 s for each batched
generation call. Each episode re-reads 2,600 prompt tokens to write 120. At that rate the main run costs
about 435 USD ([budget.md](budget.md)). The work, in order:

| | change | why it should pay | changes outputs? |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Hold inference weights in bfloat16 after merging the adapter | Students were kept in float32 under autocast, which casts 1.5 billion weights on every decoding step. The 7B reference model, loaded in bfloat16, ran at 0.43 of the 1.5B student's tokens per second though it is about five times larger | rounding only |
| R2 | Keep each episode's key-value cache between turns | a turn then reads only the new observation, not the whole conversation | rounding only |
| R3 | Size batches by tokens, not by a fixed episode count | Experiment 1 lost time to out-of-memory retries at 128 and under-filled the GPU at 32 | rounding only |
| R4 | Stop a practice attempt at its first disqualifying error | such an attempt cannot be accepted | no accepted episode changes |
| R5 | Fallback: serve sampled practice rollouts with vLLM | continuous batching and prefix caching | practice only; greedy scoring stays on the in-repo engine |

R1's explanation is an inference from Experiment 1's timings, not a measurement; Stage 1 measures it.

**Acceptance.** On CPU with the tiny test model: cached decoding equals full re-encoding token for
token, in batches of mixed length and with episodes finishing at different turns; a stopped attempt is
never accepted. On the GPU (Stage 1): the own-family tasks of `e1_s15_D1600_r0` and `e1_s15_D25_r0` (400
each, not sealed) are scored again with the new engine and compared with the committed Experiment 1 rows.
**Pass: at most 0.36 s per episode (four times faster), at most 1% of episodes changing outcome, and
success within 1 point.** Greedy scoring of sealed splits uses R1-R3 only if this passes; otherwise it
stays on the Experiment 1 path.

Sampling is seeded per task and attempt, and every attempt's trace is stored, so a resumed job never
changes which episodes a buffer holds (the Experiment 1 sampler draws from one global generator and
would).

## 5. Independent verification

The reference-model gate of Experiment 1 could vouch for shallow families only. Three layers, none of
which uses a model as judge:

**Tasks.**
- The review's selection check re-derives every list selection from the task's world: 0 failures in 5,436
  selections on the six evaluation files and in 20,204 on a practice sample. It runs on every stream task.
- 74 `test_comp` tasks were never solved by any trained run of Experiment 1. The 26 of at most three hops
  were read in review; none is defective. The 48 four-hop ones are read before Stage 3 scores a sealed
  split. A task found defective is **not** removed (the files are frozen and comparability with Experiment
  1 matters more); the primary analysis uses all 1,200 tasks and a sensitivity analysis without the
  defective ones is reported.
- A teacher is admitted only through the qualification of Stage 1, on practice tasks.

**Accepted practice episodes.**
- Replay on CPU in a separate process before entering a buffer (§3.5, step 6): guards against a fault in
  the GPU rollout path being mistaken for a success.
- **Verifier audit.** After the pilot and at the end of each practice arm: a random sample of 200 accepted
  episodes, half of them from those that are not strictly successful (an unnecessary or
  dependency-unsafe call), weighted back to the population. Each is compared programmatically with the
  oracle's calls and answer, and read where they differ. A **false accept** is an accepted episode that
  (i) states a requested fact for the wrong record or among other candidates' values, (ii) reached a
  required record by an identifier it had not observed, or (iii) passed through a fault of the validator
  or simulator. Pass: at most 4 in 200 (2%).
- The pre-specified warning sign of the proposal stands: accepted-but-not-strict episodes rising by more
  than 10 points from one cycle to the next stops the run for inspection.

**Results.**
- One final adapter per main arm is scored a second time on `test_comp` in a separate job; at most 1% of
  episodes may differ.
- The confirmatory contrasts are computed twice, by `scripts/s2_analyze.py` and by separate code in
  `scripts/s2_review.py`, and must agree to 0.1 point on arm means.
- Every access to a sealed split is logged, once per final adapter. `dev` is the only split scored during
  a run.
- The report is reviewed, as Experiments 0 and 1 were, by a session that did not write the analysis.

## 6. Measurement and analysis

**Primary endpoint.** Success of the final adapter on `test_comp` (600 families, 2 tasks each).

**Confirmatory contrasts.** H2(a), H2(b), H3, H4 as in §2, paired on replicate, family and task; H4 on
replicates 0-2. Two intervals are computed for each: the hierarchical bootstrap of Experiment 1 (10,000
resamples of replicates, then families, then tasks) and a Student-t interval over the replicate-level
paired differences. **A criterion is met only if both intervals meet it.** The review of Experiment 1
found the bootstrap alone too narrow at three replicates. Multiplicity: Holm over H2, H3 and H4; H2 is
represented by the weaker of its two parts.

**Secondary, descriptive.** `test_depth`, `test_tool`, `test_domain`, `test_id`, the v1 anchor; the share
of the oracle's gain achieved; strict success; every endpoint by number of hops **and by number of list
selections** (which the review found to track difficulty more closely than hops); learning curves on
`dev`; yield per bucket and cycle; the structural-diversity table of §3.3; the two `oracle-matched`
contrasts; sharpening against new capability (of 300 `dev` tasks, those the final adapter solves greedily
that the seed adapter failed in 16 sampled attempts); the verifier audit; GPU-seconds per point gained.
`test_depth` is reported with the share of episodes ended by the eight-turn limit, which the oracle
itself needs in full on 14% of its tasks.

**Replicates.** Five per main arm. After Stage 3, the pooled within-arm standard deviation of replicate
means on `test_comp` is computed without reference to differences between arms. If it exceeds 0.03 (the
threshold pre-registered in the Experiment 1 plan), replicates 5-7 of the main arms are run. Experiment 1's
estimate for this student is 0.015 to 0.022, from three replicates, which does not settle it.

**Not to be changed after any `dev` result of a trained Experiment 2 adapter is seen:** arms, stream and
seed-set construction, acceptance rule, curriculum formula and buckets, training settings, endpoints,
thresholds, the replicate rule.

## 7. Stages, caps and stop/go criteria

Each stage needs its own approval and has its own cap, enforced by the launcher with a budget entry
separate from the closed one of Experiments 0 and 1. A stage that cannot meet a criterion stops and is
reported; it is not adjusted until it passes.

| stage | what | cap (USD) | go if | otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** build | rollout engine R1-R4, practice loop, curriculum, replay validator, analysis for Experiment 2, per-experiment budget in the launcher | 0 | CPU tests pass: decoding equivalence, loop determinism and resumption, acceptance and replay, no sealed input reachable from the loop, dry run of every job spec | fix |
| **1** calibration | (a) engine on the GPU; (b) the 1.5B scoring Experiment 1 left out: `dev`, `test_depth`, `test_tool`, `test_domain` for six adapters, and the v1 anchor for the two untrained baselines; (c) seed adapter of replicate 0 and a yield probe on 400 stream tasks; (d) teacher qualification on 200 practice tasks, 50 per number of hops | 15 | (a) passes §4; (c) at least 20% of probed tasks accepted, and seed `dev` success at least 15 points below the 1,600-family adapters'; (d) see below | (a) between 2 and 4 times faster: re-cost, new approval; under 2 times: stop, try R5 once. (c) under 20%: stop; the seed is too weak for this stream and the design is revised before any main run |
| **2** pilot | replicate 0, cycles 1 and 2 of `practice` | 10 | in both cycles at least 20% of attempted tasks accepted; verifier audit at most 4 false accepts in 200; cost per cycle within twice the model's; no fall of more than 5 points on `dev` between cycles | stop and report |
| **3A** | replicates 0-2 of every arm except `teacher` | 140 | interim look at `dev` only: `practice+curriculum` at least 5 points above `seed` in the mean of three replicates | below 5 on `dev`: stop; score the sealed splits once for the adapters that exist and report H2 as not supported |
| **3B** | replicates 3-4 of `seed`, `oracle`, `practice`, `practice+curriculum`; repeat scoring | 75 | — | — |
| **4** teacher | `teacher`, replicates 0-4 | 75 | H2(a) is met, a teacher qualified in Stage 1, and its projected demonstrations cost at most 60 USD; if more, replicates 0-2 only | H2(b) reported as not tested |
| **ext.** | replicates 5-7 of the main arms | 110 | pooled replicate sd above 0.03 (§6) | not run |

**Teacher qualification (Stage 1d).** An open-weight instruct model under a licence that permits training
on its outputs, with the few-shot prompt. It qualifies if it is accepted on at least 60% of the 200 tasks
and on at least 40% of the four-hop ones, with one greedy attempt and one sampled retry. A teacher that
cannot solve deep tasks would hand the student a shallow training set and make non-inferiority to it
meaningless. No Claude output is used as training data in any arm (proposal §13).

**During any run.** A fall of more than 5 points on `dev` between cycles, or the strictness warning of §5,
stops that run for inspection. The proposal named the v1 anchor and `test_id` for this; `dev` is used so
that sealed splits are scored once.

**After Experiment 2**, the proposal's decisions stand: H2 supported, or at least half of the oracle's gain
achieved by practice, leads to Experiments 3 and 4 on the practice arm; otherwise Experiment 3 runs on
oracle data, the price of autonomy is reported, and Experiment 4 is dropped.

## 8. Cost estimate

From [`budget.md`](budget.md): unit costs as billed in Experiment 1 (A10G, 1.00 USD per hour; 3.7 s per
training step; 1.2 to 1.9 s per scored episode), 10% job overhead, 25% contingency. USD.

| | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | 3A | 3B | **Stages 1-3** | extension |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| engine as in Experiment 1, yield 40% | 12 | 8 | 273 | 141 | **435** | 207 |
| **engine 4 times faster (the Stage 1 target), yield 40%** | 12 | 3 | 107 | 52 | **174** | 77 |
| engine 4 times faster, yield 20% (the pilot's floor) | 12 | 4 | 137 | 72 | **225** | 107 |
| engine 10 times faster, yield 40% | 12 | 1 | 74 | 35 | **122** | 51 |

Stage 4: about 13 USD for the five teacher-arm adapters, and 40 to 180 USD for the demonstrations (24 to
108 for three replicates). That range is the least certain figure here; Stage 1 replaces it with a
measured cost per accepted demonstration.

What this says:

- **The proposal's 110 USD does not buy this experiment on the 1.5B student.** The planning figure for
  Stages 1-3 is 174 USD, with 225 as the bad case the gates still allow, and the teacher arm on top.
- **Faster rollouts are necessary and not sufficient.** At the Stage 1 target a practice replicate costs
  about 4.9 USD in rollouts and 4.7 USD in training, because the loop retrains from the base model every
  cycle (3,120 steps against 820 for a static arm). Ten times faster rollouts take the total from 174 to
  122, no lower.
- The largest saving that keeps every hypothesis is already in the staging: 3A is a complete small
  experiment, and 3B, Stage 4 and the extension are each spent only if the previous result warrants it.

Options that cost less, and what each gives up:

| option | saves (USD, at the target) | gives up |
|---|---|---|
| drop `practice+curriculum`; test H2 on `practice` | about 54 | H3; changes the arm H2 was pre-registered on |
| stop after 3A | about 52 | confirmatory inference for anything but large effects (three replicates) |
| one pass instead of two for a practice arm's intermediate adapters | about 21 | weaker rollout policies in cycles 2-6; departs from the loop as proposed |
| checkpoint gradients only for long batches (same gradients) | about 10-15, to be measured | nothing; an engineering item for Stage 0 |

I recommend none of the first three now. Approve Stages 0 and 1; set the main budget when Stage 1 has
measured the engine, the yield and the teacher.

## 9. Threats specific to this design

| threat | control |
|---|---|
| practice learns from false accepts | replay, verifier audit with a 2% gate, strict success beside success, the strictness warning |
| practice's accepted set is shallow, so it is compared on different tasks | one stream for all arms; the diversity table; `oracle-matched` |
| gains come from more optimisation or self-distillation | `practice-unverified`; identical final training |
| a weak teacher flatters practice | qualification with a four-hop floor; `oracle` as the bound on demonstrations |
| the seed is too strong or too weak for anything to be measured | Stage 1(c) checks both before the pilot |
| difficulty is not depth alone | endpoints also by number of list selections; curriculum buckets include shape |
| three- to six-hop test tasks were never vouched for by a reference model | the selection check at every depth; reading of all never-solved tasks; sensitivity analysis |
| seed-to-seed spread larger than three replicates suggested | both intervals required; the pooled-variance rule for replicates 5-7 |
| evaluation drifts when the engine changes | flip-rate gate against Experiment 1's own rows; repeat scoring |
| result specific to one model family | not addressed here; the proposal's second family is a later, separately approved stage |
